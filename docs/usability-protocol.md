# Dry usability pilot protocol

[Home](../README.md) · [Prototype](prototype.md) · [Observation form](observation-form.md)

Version 0.1 — November 8, 2026. Proposed; not conducted. Recruitment is not authorized by this document alone.

Scope: earlier adult boating research, now a secondary track. This is not a protocol for the highest-priority [child water-approach warning](child-water-proximity-use-case.md).

## Purpose and design

Explore whether the preparation companion changes safety-relevant omissions and wrong decisions, and identify comfort problems in unmodified equipment. Recruit a proposed formative sample of 12 adults fitting the equipment's prescribed user limits and relevant lake boating/canoeing scenario. Record boating experience and prior inflatable/beacon familiarity without collecting unnecessary personal data.

Randomly allocate six people to each arm using a shuffled list of six A and six B assignments, prepared before sessions. A receives the reference equipment and manufacturer instructions. B receives identical equipment/instructions plus the companion. Keep task wording, equipment setup, instruction access, time limits, room, and facilitator script the same. Record deviations. This small exploratory comparison cannot establish statistical reliability, safety performance, or lives saved.

## Preconditions before any session

- Fully review and reconcile the source set in the prototype brief. Freeze document revisions for both arms.
- Build an answer key for each task with an exact official section/page, expected action, permitted alternatives, and safety relevance. Have a qualified reviewer resolve ambiguous requirements. Do not score unpublished assumptions as manufacturer requirements.
- Prepare matching photos, inert training equipment, or paper mock-ups for faults and beacon operations. Never introduce faults into wearable safety equipment or use live distress transmissions.
- Confirm the reference equipment is suitable and unmodified, document the dry setting and stop procedure, and obtain voluntary informed consent. Explain withdrawal without penalty; decide retention/access/deletion practices before collecting records.
- Trial the facilitator script and timing without recruited participants; adjust and freeze the protocol. This has not yet happened.

## Session sequence

1. Explain that this is a document/usability study, not an equipment certification or water test. Assign anonymous ID and record arm and experience.
2. Ask the participant to find the correct instructions for the specified model pair and identify applicable installation/service versus pre-outing checks.
3. Present the same staged examples in the same order: mismatched model instructions, uncertain installation evidence, a service record needing review, and a beacon-state illustration requiring interpretation. Final details and correct answers depend on the verified manuals. Ask what they would do and why; use inert representations only.
4. Ask them to fit the unmodified vest according to its instructions. Observe without coaching unless intervention is needed; record the intervention. Do not activate the beacon or inflate the vest as a study task.
5. Conduct dry seated and gentle paddling motions with a stable seat, clear surroundings, and no water exposure. Record neck pressure, arm restriction, seat interference, heat, and adjustment access. Stop for pain, distress, or participant request.
6. Ask what each status means and whether any entry proves the equipment is safe or guarantees rescue. Ask which instructions were hard to find. Debrief misunderstandings using the official sources; do not leave participants with a false readiness assurance.

## Outcomes and analysis

Primary: per-participant safety-relevant omissions and wrong decisions, judged against the prereviewed answer key. Record task-level opportunities as well as counts, distinguish omission from incorrect action, and retain the source reference and observation supporting each judgment. Record correct identification of uncertainty separately from unsupported confirmation. If an answer is ambiguous, label it unresolved for review rather than forcing a score.

Secondary: elapsed time per task, requests for help, facilitator interventions, status misunderstanding, and comfort observations. Use consistent comfort ratings: 0 none, 1 mild, 2 moderate, 3 severe; adjustment access: easy/difficult/unable. Record clothing and seat configuration because they affect interpretation.

Report counts with denominators, individual patterns, and ranges by arm; do not infer a causal safety benefit or population reliability. Summarize differences in experience and any protocol deviations. A second reviewer should check safety-relevant coding against the frozen answer key where feasible; record disagreements and resolution.

Any feature that encourages false confidence, obscures a required check, or confuses installation with routine pre-use work must be revised or removed before further evaluation. Improved speed alone is insufficient. Only investigate physical modifications after identifying a specific unmet need. No recruitment, observations, or results exist yet.
